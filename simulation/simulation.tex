A realistic simulation environment is created using the setup defined in chapter \ref{chap:modelling}, and the results of the simulations are presented.

\section{Experimental Design}
In Chapter \ref{chap:modelling} the EOSSAT-1 orbit was defined and it's un-synchronous orbit poses some unique challenges for the underactuated controller. Typical Earth Observation satellites operations involve a combination of nadir and sun pointing. In this section, these operations are defined and their implications for the underactuated controller are discussed. Given this information, choices and constraints for the experimental setup are discussed and metrics for evaluating the performance of the underactuated controller are defined. 

\subsection{Satellite Operations}

The two main important operations are important for Earth Observation satellites: imaging and sun pointing. During imaging, the satellite's camera is pointed towards the Earth to capture images sometimes at nadir and other times at other roll angles relative to nadir. During sun pointing  the satellite's solar panels are pointed towards the Sun to maximize power generation. At 500km altitude, EOSSAT-1 will spend roughly half of its orbit in eclipse and the other half in sunlight. Typically the satellite will spend the sunlit portion of the orbit sun pointing with short bursts of nadir pointing for imaging. During eclipse any orientation is possible as no specific orientation is required, however a common method of operating is to point nadir. This is convienient for the experimental setup as it allows for a simple and repeatable scenario where the satellite is required to switch between nadir and sun pointing which will give an indication for the performance of the underactuated controller in a realistic scenario.

For the purposes of this simulation, two additional reference frames are defined: the nadir pointing frame $F_N$ and the sun pointing frame $F_S$. The nadir pointing frame is defined such that the $z$-axis points towards the center of the Earth, the $x$-axis points in the direction of motion, and the $y$-axis completes the right-handed coordinate system. The sun pointing frame is defined such that the $z$-axis points towards the Sun, the $x$-axis points in the direction of motion, and the $y$-axis completes the right-handed coordinate system. The orientation of these frames relative to the body frame is defined by a set of Euler angles which are used to define the desired attitude for each operation.
