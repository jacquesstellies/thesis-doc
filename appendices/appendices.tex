The choice of the oscillatory term given by equation \ref{eq:oscillator} is not immediately obvious.  First we note that it is given that 
\begin{align*}
    \frac{d \vec{e}_d^T \vec{e}_d}{dt} &= \vec{e}_d^T \Big[\frac{\dot\xi}{\xi}\vec{e}_d + \Lambda\vec{G}_1\vec{e}_d \Big] \\
    &=2\vec{e}_d^T\frac{\dot\xi}{\xi}\vec{e}_d + 2\vec{e}_d^T\Lambda\vec{G}_1\vec{e}_d \\
    &=2\vec{e}_d^T\frac{\dot\xi}{\xi}\vec{e}_d \numberthis \label{eq:oscillator_norm_ode}
\end{align*}
since $G_1 = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ is skew-symmetric then it has the property where any vector $\vec{x}$ satisfies $\vec{x}^T\vec{G}_1\vec{x} = 0$ and $\Lambda$ can be treated as a scalar.


Given equation \ref{eq:oscillator_norm_ode}, let $r(t)=e_d(t)$, so 

\begin{align*}
    \frac{dr}{dt} &= 2\frac{\dot{\xi}}{\xi}r \\
    \frac{dr}{dt} \frac{1}{r} &= 2\frac{d\xi}{dt}\frac{1}{\xi} \\
\end{align*}

valid since $r>0$, guaranteed as long as $\xi>0$, which the $\gamma_2>0$ condition guarantees. Integrating from $0$ to $t$ gives
$$\ln r(t)-\ln r(0) = 2\ln\xi(t)-2\ln\xi(0) \Longrightarrow r(t) = r(0)\cdot\frac{\xi(t)}{\xi(0)}$$

6. Apply the matched initial condition
The design pins $r(0)=|e_d(0)|=\xi(0)$ (that's what $e_d^T(0)e_d(0)=\xi^2(0)$ means). Substituting:
$$r(t) = \xi(0)\cdot\frac{\xi(t)}{\xi(0)} = \xi(t)\qquad\text{for all }t\ge0$$
